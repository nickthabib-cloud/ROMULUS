**Do the packet leads match gold?** Yes, both are Tier A.

- The `/TODO.md` lead cites 9 Arkime sessions. Each is a gold `action` locator of the stager-delivery opportunities: the first download, by the root cron job that the attacker edited, and the 8 recurring 15-minute re-downloads. The lead's entities (attacker address and video server) and its time range satisfy §6.
- The `/README.txt.1` lead cites 1 Arkime session. That session is a gold `action` locator of the SSH authorized-key persistence opportunity.

**Did they turn a miss or a lead-only item into a hit?** No.

- All 3 opportunities were already Tier A in Reported in run 8 from host-index evidence, and already had Arkime and network-index locators among their surfaced hits.
- The packet leads are in the `lead` lane, so they could not have raised reported recall even on an opportunity that was not yet hit.
- They add a content-level corroboration that earlier runs lacked: the served body itself. The scorer does not yet count that separately from Arkime, because their citations canonicalize to the Arkime session key (PROTOCOL §13).
- `host.ssh_authorized_keys_write` again did not fire: the key append ran inside a downloaded script, not in an argv. The packet lead is the only item that reflects the key-append content, and it is a lead.

**Did the audit-auth change alter any finding?** It changed citations only. Lanes, verdicts and recall did not change.

- Both `host.ssh_login_external_source` items now also cite auditd `USER_LOGIN`/`USER_START` records, which is why their signatures changed. The attacker's root login is still a Tier A hit in the `lead` lane.
- The remote-login opportunity is still a surfaced-only miss. Its promotion rule (`context.entry_point_promotion`) runs only under expansion, which was disabled.
- The lab log-collection item was re-adjudicated `benign` against raw telemetry: the same account, source address and host as the earlier benign verdict.
- `ioc.indicator_match` produced no item from `auth` events. All 3 of its items came from `process_exec`.
- One semantic point for the lead: the benign item's claim counts "40 successful SSH authentication record(s)". That count now includes PAM `USER_START` session-open records alongside `Accepted publickey` and `USER_LOGIN`, so it overstates the number of logins. The item also cites 20 of those records, and the claim's time range extends past the last cited record.

There are 3 locator-match anomalies, against 1 in run 8. All 3 are the known limitation in PROTOCOL §13: an enumeration-script exec record is `indirect` evidence for the inferable-only tool-transfer opportunity, and it falls outside that opportunity's time window. Two of the three are the new `ioc.indicator_match` items, and the third is the same `host.web_service_spawns_interpreter` lead as in run 8. None is a hit, and none changes a verdict.

## Caveats

- One run per arm, n = 22 observable opportunities. The intervals are wide.
- **Packet-stage citations** use a new locator form (`arkime-pcap:<index>`, `<sessions3 _id>@<response epoch>`). The scorer was extended to canonicalize them to the Arkime session key (PROTOCOL §13, 2026-10-20). The change touches only that form, so no earlier run's score changes.
- **Hunt state `partial`.** The packet summary does not list the 2 unrebuilt sessions, so the custodian cannot say whether they intersect gold evidence. The brief describes them as ZoneMinder streaming responses.
- **No redeploy.** Scored from a copy of the `eval/` working tree on the VM, with the packet-citation change applied. The app was at `8baae62`.
- **Review order:** neither run has a report-manifest revision, so the replicated manifest sort is used.
