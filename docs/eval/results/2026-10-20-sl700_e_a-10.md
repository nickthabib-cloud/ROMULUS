### Why every `login_session` link is unsupported

**16 anchors genuinely have no audit session.** 14 are www-data processes, whose audit session is unset, and 2 are network-only.

**The other 6 should have had a usable session key.** They are root processes: 3 in a cron-launched session and 3 in the attacker's SSH login session.

- Their raw auditd SYSCALL records carry a real audit session.
- The stored typed events they cite have no `process.session_id`.

**The cause is stale typed events.**

- Those events were first written by hunts that ran before session extraction was added to the audit normalizer (commits `6efcb89` and `696a85c`).
- The normalizer version string did not change (`lab-ecs@1`).
- The store inserts typed events with ignore-on-conflict.
- Expansion builds its anchors from stored rows (`ExpansionEngine._events` → `Repo.events_by_ids`).

**Today's normalizer gives the right key.** Replaying it over the same raw records yields the session ID, with the same event IDs.

**Consequences:**

- Expansion output depends on store history.
- The determinism twin hunt cannot reveal this, because it read the same stale rows.
- A hunt on a fresh store would expand differently.

### Counterfactual

This is the custodian's replay, not ROMULUS output. With fresh session keys, the `login_session` links of the 3 SSH-session anchors would cover these records:

- **All 4 discovery steps that missed at `evaluated`.** Under today's `context.post_access_discovery`, 1 of the 4 uses a command on the rule's list and 3 use commands outside it.
- **The remote-login entry.** It would still not be promoted. In this non-interactive session, the only sshd success record carrying the session key is the PAM session-open record, and `context.entry_point_promotion` skips that record by design. No USER_LOGIN record exists for this session.

The expected effect of fixing the stale rows alone is at most +1 reported opportunity (discovery, low).
